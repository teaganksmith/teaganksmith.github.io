Evidence of Purifying Selection in Cytokine Receptor 2 (CXCR2) Reveals Differences in Cetacean Rate of Protein Evolution

EEB460, University of Toronto · Toronto, ON · 2023

Is there a quantifiable difference in CXCR2 protein evolution rates across cetaceans, and does purifying selection on this immune receptor contribute to reduced cancer incidence in large-bodied baleen whales?

My Role

I designed and executed the full analysis independently: sequence acquisition, alignment, phylogenetic reconstruction, and evolutionary selection analysis across a 20-species cetacean dataset.

Methods

CXCR2 amino acid sequences were obtained from NCBI and Ensembl and aligned using ClustalO. The dataset included 17 cetacean species and 3 artiodactyl outgroups. Maximum likelihood phylogenetic reconstruction was performed in MEGA using the JTT + G model selected across BIC, AICc, and parameter number calculations, with bootstrap support from 500 replicates. Branch length analysis used Mr. Bayes phylogenetic reconstruction configured with GTR + G + I substitution rates and Blosum62 likelihood model, run for 10,000 MCMC generations. Trees were visualized in FigTree. Evolutionary selection analysis was attempted in PAML using random site models and branch-site models to assess dN/dS rates and positive selection.

Key Findings

Mysticetes showed significantly shorter CXCR2 branch lengths than most Odontocetes, indicating slower rates of amino acid substitution. Delphinidae and Inioidea showed the highest substitution rates. Results support the hypothesis that purifying selection acts on CXCR2 in large-bodied baleen whales, potentially suppressing tumorigenesis pathways consistent with Peto's Paradox. PAML dN/dS analysis was limited by stop codons in predicted codon sequences, constraining conclusions on directional selection.
