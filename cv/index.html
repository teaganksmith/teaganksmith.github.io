Research Experience

Quantifying the Frequency and Distribution of Beluga Whale Scars Through Photo Identification in the Churchill River July – August 2023

Arctic Field Biology Course, University of Toronto Mississauga & University of Manitoba · Churchill, MB

Supervisors: Peter Kotanen, Stephen Peterson

Independent field research assessing anthropogenic injury to the Churchill River beluga subpopulation. Conducted ten boat surveys, photographed surfacing pods, and analysed 5,642 photos from a 10,239-image catalogue using a seven-segment body classification system developed from existing literature. Of scarred adults, 63.63% carried presumed anthropogenic scars, with entanglement as the most frequent source. Scar distribution varied significantly across body segments (p = 0.005). Produced the beluga body segment illustration used in the study. Collaborated with Beluga Bits citizen science initiative, Assiniboine Park Zoo, and Polar Bear International.

The Biogeography and Carbon Footprint of Coral Reef Science November 2020 – April 2021

Darling Lab, Department of Ecology & Evolutionary Biology, University of Toronto · Toronto, ON

Supervisor: Dr. Emily Darling

Analysed geographic patterns in coral reef science production and research travel using reef health data pulled from MERMAID and integrated with a coral reef publications dataset in R Studio. Used tidyverse to calculate unique travel routes, countries represented, mean distance traveled, and estimated carbon emissions (tCO2e). Results visualised using ggplot2, linking publication records to field site locations and researcher institutional affiliations across biogeographic regions.

Supervisor: Dr. Tammy Sage

Selected Writing

Evidence of Purifying Selection in Cytokine Receptor 2 (CXCR2) Reveals Differences in Cetacean Rate of Protein Evolution December 2023

EEB460H1F, University of Toronto

Investigated CXCR2 protein evolution across 20 cetacean and artiodactyl species to assess whether purifying selection on this immune receptor contributes to reduced cancer incidence in large-bodied baleen whales. Acquired sequences from NCBI and Ensembl, aligned with ClustalO, and reconstructed phylogenies using maximum likelihood in MEGA (JTT + G model, 500 bootstrap replicates) and Bayesian inference in Mr. Bayes (GTR + G + I, Blosum62, 10,000 MCMC generations). Trees visualised in FigTree. Results supported purifying selection acting on Mysticete CXCR2, consistent with Peto's Paradox.
