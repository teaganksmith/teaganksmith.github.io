Art

I make oil paintings and biological illustrations that grow out of the same questions my research asks: about the sublime, about ecological relationship and loss, and about what it means to pay close attention to the living world at a moment when so much of it is disappearing.

The oil paintings are concerned with climate grief. I am interested in the macabre register of the sublime, the awe and fear of nature as something far more powerful than us. The biological illustrations work differently, attending to form, texture, and the logic of an organism's structure.

I want this work to make you feel something you have been avoiding. Looking directly at it, holding both the grief and the possibility of doing something, is exactly the antidote to the apathy that follows fear.
